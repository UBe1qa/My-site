Damiano Defense (2...f6): Why It Fails and How to Punish It

2...f6 guards e5 with a pawn, but it takes the best square from the g8 knight and opens the e8–h5 diagonal. It is a classic beginner move, and it scores badly for Black at every level below 2000.

Plans for both sides

White

  • 3.Nxe5! is the punishment: if 3...fxe5, then 4.Qh5+ wins material or mates.
  • Against 3...Qe7 retreat with 4.Nf3. Black wins the pawn back with ...Qxe4+ but loses time, and White develops with Be2 and O-O.
  • Quiet moves like 3.Bc4 also give a nice game: Black cannot castle short easily with the f-pawn on f6.

Black

  • If you play 2...f6, never take back with 3...fxe5 after 3.Nxe5.
  • 3...Qe7 4.Nf3 Qxe4+ 5.Be2 is the least bad line, but White is clearly better.
  • The simple fix is 2...Nc6: it guards e5 and develops a piece.

Common traps

3...fxe5? 4.Qh5+ g6

After 4...g6 5.Qxe5+ the queen forks the king and the rook on h8. White wins the rook.

In our data, the last move of this line was played in 727 games.

Show on the board: 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 f6 3.Nxe5 fxe5 4.Qh5+ g6 5.Qxe5+ Qe7 6.Qxh8

3...fxe5? 4.Qh5+ Ke7

The king walk is worse: White keeps checking, brings the h-pawn and rook into the attack and mates on h5.

Fewer than 6 games in our data reached the end of this line.
