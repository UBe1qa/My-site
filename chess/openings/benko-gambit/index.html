Benko Gambit (3...b5): Accepted, Declined and Stats by Rating

3...b5 gives a pawn for lasting pressure on the a- and b-files. After 4.cxb5 a6 5.bxa6 Bxa6 Black fianchettoes, castles and puts the rooks on a8 and b8. The pressure does not go away when pieces are traded, which is why Benko players like endgames.

Plans for both sides

White

  • The main line is 4.cxb5 a6 5.bxa6, then Nc3 and e4; after ...Bxf1 Kxf1 the king walks to g2 behind g3.
  • 5.b6 gives the pawn back at once; 5.e3 keeps it for now and aims for a quieter game.
  • 4.Nf3 or 4.a4 declines. 4.a4 hits b5 and invites ...b4, which keeps the queenside closed.

Black

  • ...Bxa6, ...g6, ...Bg7, ...d6 and ...O-O, then ...Qa5 and the rooks on a8 and b8.
  • Trade pieces happily: the pressure on a2 and b2 stays.
  • The Blumenfeld (2...e6 3.Nf3 c5 4.d5 b5) is a cousin: Black gives the b-pawn to build a big center with ...e5 or ...d5.

At club level

At 2000+ about seven in ten take the pawn with 4.cxb5, and Black still does well: White makes only about 50% after 3...b5 there. Below 1200 the Benko is rare. After 4...a6, only about half of White players take again with 5.bxa6.
