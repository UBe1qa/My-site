    For stronger players

    3...Bb4+ is the Nimzo-Indian bishop move played against 3.Nf3: with no knight on c3 yet, it comes with check. White blocks with the bishop or the knight, and Black usually trades the bishop or drops it back once White has spent time. It is a quiet, solid choice.

    Below 2000, 4...Bxd2+ is the most common answer to 4.Bd2, chosen by about two in three players at 1200 to 1599. At 2000+ 4...Qe7 takes over at about 39%, and 4.Nbd2 rises to about three in ten. White scores about 53% after 3...Bb4+.

    What now: plans for both sides

    White

    • 4.Bd2 is the main move; against 4...Qe7, g3, Bg2 and O-O give a pleasant space edge.
    • 4.Nbd2 keeps the bishops and prepares a3 to win the bishop pair.
    • 4.Nc3 turns the game into a Nimzo-Indian.

    Black

    • 4...Qe7 holds the b4 bishop and prepares ...Nc6 and ...e5 or ...d6.
    • 4...Bxd2+ is the simplest: trade the bishop and play ...d5 or ...d6 for an easy game.
    • 4...a5 keeps the bishop on b4 and prepares a ...d5 setup.
