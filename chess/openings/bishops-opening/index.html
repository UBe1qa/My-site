    For stronger players

    2.Bc4 develops the bishop before the knight. It keeps the f-pawn free for f4 and lets White transpose into the Italian, the Vienna or a King’s Gambit with the bishop already out.

    Below 1200 the Bishop’s Opening is often the start of a Scholar’s mate attempt (Qh5 or Qf3). As Black, ...g6 or ...Qe7 stops it; the trap below is the pattern to recognize.

    Traps and how to punish them

    Scholar’s mate

    With the bishop on c4 and the queen on h5, f7 is attacked twice. The natural 3...Nf6?? attacks the queen but allows 4.Qxf7 mate. Defend first with 3...g6 or 3...Qe7.

    In our data, the last move of this line was played in 21k games.

    Show on the board: 1.e4 e5 2.Bc4 Nc6 3.Qh5 Nf6 4.Qxf7#(ends in mate)

    What now: plans for both sides

    White

    • Against 2...Nf6, 3.d3 is calm and 3.d4 is the sharp Urusov Gambit.
    • Nc3 and f4 give a Vienna-style attack; Nf3 gives an Italian.
    • The bishop on c4 and a queen on h5 or f3 target f7, the pattern behind Scholar’s mate.

    Black

    • 2...Nf6 is the most testing reply and attacks e4 at once.
    • Develop knights before bishops, and when a queen and bishop both aim at f7, defend it first.
    • ...c6 and ...d5 is a good plan to hit the c4 bishop with tempo.
