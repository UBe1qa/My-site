    For stronger players

    2.Nc3 and 3.Bg5 aim for e4 with pieces instead of c4 with a pawn. White often doubles Black’s pawns with Bxf6, castles long and attacks. It sidesteps Queen’s Gambit theory and leads to unbalanced positions.

    Below 1200, 3...Nc6 and 3...h6 are common replies, and three in five White players answer 3...Bf5 with 4.Bxf6. At 2000+ three in four answer 3...e6 with 4.e4, and half meet 3...Bf5 with 4.f3. White scores about 51% overall.

    What now: plans for both sides

    White

    • Against 3...e6, 4.e4 reaches a French Classical (the 4.Bg5 line) and avoids the Winawer and other French systems.
    • Against 3...Bf5, 4.f3 prepares e4; 4.Bxf6 doubles the pawns at once.
    • f3, e4, Qd2 and O-O-O is the aggressive plan when Black allows it.

    Black

    • 3...e6 is the most common reply; after 4.e4, 4...Be7 or 4...dxe4 keeps things solid.
    • 3...Bf5 develops first; if White plays Bxf6, take back with the e- or g-pawn and use the bishop pair.
    • 3...Nbd7 prepares ...c5 or ...h6 and keeps the pawns healthy if White takes on f6.
