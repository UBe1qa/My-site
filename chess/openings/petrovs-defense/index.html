Plans for both sides

White

  • 3.Nxe5 is the main move. After 3...d6 retreat with 4.Nf3, and Black takes back the pawn with 4...Nxe4.
  • 3.Nc3 (Three Knights) and 3.d4 avoid the symmetrical main lines.
  • In the main line, d4, Bd3 and c4 put pressure on Black’s knight on e4.

Black

  • After 3.Nxe5 play 3...d6 first, then ...Nxe4. Never take on e4 at once.
  • Keep the knight on e4 supported with ...d5 and ...Bd6.
  • Black aims for a safe, equal position; it is a good choice if you want to avoid sharp theory.

Common traps

3...Nxe4? 4.Qe2!

Copying White with 3...Nxe4? runs into 4.Qe2. If the knight retreats with 4...Nf6??, 5.Nc6+ is a discovered check that wins the queen. Even 4...Qe7 5.Qxe4 d6 6.d4 leaves Black a pawn down.

At club level

The single most important thing to know: 3.Nxe5 Nxe4? 4.Qe2! and Black is already in trouble. Under 1200, 3...Nxe4? is actually the most common reply. The other line to know is the Stafford Gambit (3...Nc6), which is popular online because of its traps.
