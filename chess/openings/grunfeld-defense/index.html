Grünfeld Defense: Exchange, Russian, 4.Bf4 and Stats by Rating

3...d5 lets White build a big pawn center and then attacks it. After 4.cxd5 Nxd5 5.e4 Nxc3 6.bxc3 Bg7 White has pawns on c3, d4 and e4, and Black hits d4 with the g7 bishop, ...c5 and ...Nc6. It is sharp, deeply analyzed and a top-level favorite.

Plans for both sides

White

  • The Exchange Variation (4.cxd5 and 5.e4) takes the center; support d4 with Nf3 and Be3, or with Bc4 and Ne2.
  • 4.Nf3 with Qb3 (the Russian), Bg5 or Bf4 keeps more tension and avoids the main Exchange theory.
  • Hold d4 at all costs: if it falls, Black’s g7 bishop takes over the long diagonal.

Black

  • Attack d4 with ...Bg7, ...c5, ...Nc6, ...Bg4 and ...Qa5.
  • Castle quickly; the g7 bishop is both your best attacker and your king’s guard.
  • Against 4.Bg5, 4...Ne4 hits the bishop at once and is the most common answer at 2000+.

At club level

At 2000+ half of White players answer 3...d5 with 4.cxd5 and the big center, while below 1200 4.Nf3 is more common. The Grünfeld is about three in ten 3.Nc3 games at 2000+ (3...d5 at 28% against 3...Bg7 at 68%), and White scores only about 50% after it.
