Plans for both sides

White

  • Bxc6 is often the point: doubled c-pawns and a slow game where your knights can outplay Black’s bishops.
  • O-O, Re1 and c3 with d4 is the other plan, keeping the bishop for now.
  • Against 3...g6, 4.Bxc6 dxc6 5.d3 with h3, Nc3 and Be3 is a well-known setup.

Black

  • 3...g6 is the most common answer at 2000+: the bishop goes to g7, where it backs up the c5 pawn’s hold on d4 and eyes the long diagonal.
  • After Bxc6 you can take back with ...bxc6 toward the center or ...dxc6 to free your pieces; both are playable.
  • 3...e6 prepares ...Nge7 so that a knight can take back on c6.

At club level

Below 1200 the most common answer to 3.Bb5 is 3...a6, asking the bishop to decide at once. At 2000+ a third choose 3...g6, and after it half see 4.O-O and over a third 4.Bxc6. Below 2000 White takes on c6 more often than castling there.
