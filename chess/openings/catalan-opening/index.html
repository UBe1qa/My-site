Catalan Opening: Open and Closed Lines, Plans and Stats by Rating

The Catalan puts a bishop on g2 behind a Queen’s Gambit center. Its target is the long diagonal toward b7 and Black’s queenside. It is a positional opening: White often gives a pawn on c4 for pressure that lasts deep into the middlegame.

Plans for both sides

White

  • g3, Bg2, Nf3 and O-O first; the c4 pawn can be won back later with Qc2, Qa4+ or Ne5.
  • Against the Open Catalan (...dxc4), do not rush to regain the pawn: development and the g2 bishop matter more.
  • In the Closed Catalan, play Qc2, Nbd2 and e4 to open the center for your pieces.

Black

  • 4...dxc4 takes the pawn; ...a6 and ...b5 can hold it, or Black gives it back for easy development.
  • 4...Be7 with ...O-O and ...c6 is the Closed Catalan: solid but passive.
  • The c8 bishop is Black’s problem piece; plan ...b6 and ...Bb7, or ...c5, to free it.

At club level

The Catalan is mostly an expert’s opening here: about two in three games with 3.g3 are played at 2000+. White scores about 54% overall, with draws near 13% at 2000+, and after 4.Bg2 Black there chooses 4...Be7 most often (about 39%), with 4...dxc4 and 4...c6 near 17% each.
