Wayward Queen Attack (2.Qh5) and 2.Qf3: Beating the Early Queen

2.Qh5 brings the queen out on move two to hit e5 and f7. It sets up the Scholar’s Mate with Bc4, and below 1200 it is one of the most common second moves. Defend correctly and the white queen just becomes a target.

Plans for both sides

White

  • The idea is Bc4 and Qxf7#. Against careful play, switch to development: Nf3 or Ne2, Nc3, d3 and castle.
  • After 2...Nc6 3.Bc4 g6 4.Qf3 Nf6, do not bring the queen out again; play Ne2 and Nbc3.
  • 2.Qf3 (Napoleon Attack) has the same idea and the same problem: the queen takes the f3 square from the knight.

Black

  • 2...Nc6 guards e5. After 3.Bc4 play 3...g6, which shuts the queen out of f7 and hits it (or 3...Qe7 to guard f7); after 4.Qf3, 4...Nf6 closes the f-file and develops.
  • Never answer 3.Bc4 with 3...Nf6?? — 4.Qxf7 is mate.
  • Treat the early queen as a target: develop with ...Nf6, ...Bg7 and ...d5 while it keeps moving, and Black is often better.

Common traps

Scholar’s Mate: 3...Nf6??

The knight attacks the queen, but the queen and bishop already hit f7. 4.Qxf7 is mate.
