Plans for both sides

White

  • 4.g3 is the main line: the g2 bishop meets Black’s b7 bishop on the long diagonal.
  • Against 4...Ba6, 5.b3 protects c4; after 5...Bb4+ 6.Bd2 Be7 White develops with Bg2 and O-O.
  • 4.a3 (Petrosian) stops ...Bb4 and prepares Nc3 and d5; 4.Nc3 allows ...Bb4 and a Nimzo-like game.

Black

  • Control e4: ...Bb7, ...Ne4 and sometimes ...f5 are the main ideas.
  • 4...Ba6 hits c4 at once and makes White spend a move defending it.
  • Keep the center flexible; ...d5 or ...c5 at the right moment frees your game.

At club level

This is an expert’s opening: more than three in five Queen’s Indian games here are played at 2000+, and below 1200 it is very rare. At 2000+ White answers with 4.g3 in about half the games, and Black then picks 4...Ba6 about a third of the time. White scores about 52% after 3...b6.
