1.d4 d5: Every Second Move for White, Main and Rare

1...d5 stops e4 and leads to the closed games. White’s second move decides the family: 2.c4 is the Queen’s Gambit, 2.Bf4 the London, and quiet setups with Nf3 and e3 lead to the Colle or the Stonewall. This page also covers the rare second moves that have names of their own.

Plans for both sides

White

  • 2.c4 is the most common move from 1200 up and almost half the games at 2000+; below 1200, 2.Bf4 (the London) is played more often.
  • 2.Nf3 and 2.e3 keep the London, the Colle and the Queen’s Gambit open; 2.Nc3 leads to the Veresov or the Rapport-Jobava setup.
  • 2.e4 is the Blackmar-Diemer Gambit and 2.Bg5 the Levitsky Attack, two sharp ways to avoid theory.

Black

  • Against 2.c4 choose a structure: ...e6 (Declined), ...c6 (Slav) or ...dxc4 (Accepted).
  • Against the quiet systems, ...Nf6 with ...c5 or ...Bf5 is the simplest way to equalize.
  • Against 2.Bg5, 2...h6 or 2...c6 asks the bishop what it wants; 2...f6 is playable but loosens the king.

At club level

Below 1200, 2.Nc3 and 2.e3 are each played in about one game in seven, and 2.c4 in only one in five. At 2000+ the Queen’s Gambit is close to half of all games, with 2.Nf3 next at about a quarter. White scores about 53% after 1...d5 at every rating.
