    For stronger players

    2...e5 offers a pawn on move two. After 3.dxe5 Ng4 Black wins it back with ...Nc6, ...Bb4+ and ...Qe7 in most lines, and gets quick development and tricks against an unprepared opponent.

    At 2000+ about three in four take the pawn with 3.dxe5, and then 3...Ng4 comes in about 85% of games. Below 1200 the Fajarowicz 3...Ne4 is far more common, at about one game in three. White scores about 50% after 2...e5 overall, so it is a respectable surprise.

    Traps and how to punish them

    The Nd3 mate

    After 6.Nbd2 Qe7 7.a3 Ngxe5 White should not grab the bishop: 8.axb4?? Nd3 is mate. The e2 pawn is pinned by the queen on e7, and every square around White’s king is blocked by its own pieces.

    What now: plans for both sides

    White

    • 3.dxe5 Ng4 4.Bf4 or 4.Nf3 holds the pawn for a while; give it back calmly rather than weakening yourself.
    • After ...Bb4+, block with Nbd2 and watch the d3 square: ...Ngxe5 and ...Nd3 is the classic trick.
    • 3.d5 or 3.Nf3 declines and avoids the theory.

    Black

    • 3...Ng4 is the main move; ...Nc6, ...Bb4+ and ...Qe7 win e5 back.
    • 3...Ne4 (the Fajarowicz) is trickier but leaves the knight exposed.
    • Do not hurry: the e5 pawn usually falls once you have developed.
