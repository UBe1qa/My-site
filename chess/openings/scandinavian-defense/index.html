Scandinavian Defense: Qa5, Qd6, Qd8 and 2...Nf6 by Rating

1...d5 challenges e4 at once. After 2.exd5 Qxd5 Black’s queen comes out early, but it is easy to find a safe square. You get the same kind of position against almost every White setup, which makes it a great low-theory weapon.

Plans for both sides

White

  • 3.Nc3 gains a tempo on the queen; then d4, Nf3 and Bc4 or Bd2.
  • Your pawn on d4 gives you more space; Ne5 and a quick c4 are typical ways to use it.
  • Against 2...Nf6, 3.d4 Nxd5 4.Nf3 is simple; holding the pawn with 3.c4 invites the ...c6 or ...e6 gambits.

Black

  • Choose your queen square: a5 (active), d6 (modern) or d8 (solid).
  • Develop with ...Nf6, ...c6, ...Bf5 or ...Bg4 and ...e6; this setup works against almost everything.
  • Do not let White’s pieces gain more tempi on the queen.

At club level

The Scandinavian is common below 1600 because it avoids theory. From 1200 up most White players answer 2.exd5 and 3.Nc3, so learning one queen retreat and one setup covers most of your games; under 1200, expect 2.e5 too.
