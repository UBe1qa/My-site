    For stronger players

    2...dxc4 takes the pawn, not to keep it, but to get free development while White spends a move winning it back. Black then hits the center with ...c5 or ...e5.

    A common club mistake is 3...b5 to protect the c4 pawn. It does not work, as the line below shows, and Black ends up worse.

    Traps and how to punish them

    Holding the pawn with ...b5

    After 3...b5 4.a4 c6 5.axb5 cxb5?, 6.Qf3! hits the rook on a8 along the long diagonal and Black loses material. Do not defend c4 with pawns.

    In our data, the last move of this line was played in 22k games.

    Show on the board: 1.d4 d5 2.c4 dxc4 3.e3 b5 4.a4 c6 5.axb5 cxb5 6.Qf3

    What now: plans for both sides

    White

    • 3.e4 grabs the full center; 3.e3 and 3.Nf3 are calmer.
    • Take the pawn back with Bxc4 and use your central majority.
    • If Black tries to hold the pawn with ...b5, a4 breaks the chain.

    Black

    • Do not try to keep the pawn; develop with ...Nf6, ...e6, ...c5 and ...a6.
    • Against 3.e4, strike with ...e5 or ...Nf6 and ...c5.
    • An isolated d-pawn for White is a common result; blockade it on d5.
