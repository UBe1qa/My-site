Two Knights Defense: Fried Liver, Traxler and 4.d3 by Rating

3...Nf6 is the sharp answer to the Italian. Instead of guarding e5 with a bishop, Black attacks e4 at once and dares White to go after f7 with 4.Ng5. Strong players mostly answer 4.d3 and keep it quiet; below 1200, 4.Ng5 is the most common reply.

Plans for both sides

White

  • 4.d3 is the modern main line: protect e4, castle, then c3, Re1 and Nbd2 as in the Giuoco Pianissimo.
  • 4.Ng5 hits f7 a second time. After 4...d5 5.exd5 Black cannot simply take back on d5 without allowing a strong attack.
  • 4.d4 opens the center at once. After 4...exd4 5.O-O you are in the Scotch Gambit, where quick development matters more than the pawn.

Black

  • Against 4.Ng5, 4...d5 is the main answer. After 5.exd5 play 5...Na5: you give a pawn but chase the bishop and develop fast.
  • The natural 5...Nxd5? lets White sacrifice on f7 (the Fried Liver) or play the even stronger 6.d4.
  • The Traxler, 4...Bc5!?, ignores the threat and counterattacks f2. It is a gamble; learn it only if you enjoy chaos.

Common traps

Fried Liver: 5...Nxd5?

After 6.Nxf7! Kxf7 7.Qf3+ the black king must go to e6 to keep the d5 knight, and White piles up on the pinned knight with Nc3, O-O and Re1. Over the board it is very hard to defend.

At club level

Below 1200 about two in three players answer 4.Ng5 d5 5.exd5 with the natural 5...Nxd5, and White then scores around 60%. At 2000+ two in three play 5...Na5. If you play 3...Nf6 as Black, 5...Na5 is the one line you must know.
