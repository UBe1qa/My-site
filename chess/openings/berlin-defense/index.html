    For stronger players

    3...Nf6 counterattacks e4 instead of asking the bishop a question with ...a6. Kramnik used it to beat Kasparov in their 2000 world title match, and since then it has been one of the most solid answers to the Ruy Lopez. At 2000+ about one in four of our Berlin games is drawn.

    Below 1200 the most common reply is 4.Bxc6, which gives up the bishop for nothing special. At 2000+ six in ten play 4.O-O and three in ten 4.d3, and over half of the full Berlin endgames are drawn.

    What now: plans for both sides

    White

    • 4.O-O is the main move: after 4...Nxe4 5.d4 Nd6 6.Bxc6 dxc6 7.dxe5 Nf5 8.Qxd8+ Kxd8 you reach the Berlin endgame.
    • 4.d3 (the Anti-Berlin) avoids the endgame and keeps pieces on, aiming for a slow Ruy-style middlegame.
    • 5.Re1 after 4.O-O Nxe4 is another way to avoid the endgame: White wins the pawn back and keeps queens on.

    Black

    • In the Berlin endgame Black has lost castling but has the bishop pair and a solid structure. Bring the king to e8 or c7 and coordinate the rooks.
    • Against 4.d3, play ...Bc5 or ...d6 with a normal Ruy Lopez setup, often with ...Ne7 and ...Ng6.
    • Against 4.O-O the main line is 4...Nxe4: after 5.d4 drop the knight to d6 instead of holding on to the pawn. Against 4.d3, e4 is protected, so just develop.
