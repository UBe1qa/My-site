    For stronger players

    3...c5 hits White’s center at once. After 4.cxd5 exd5 Black usually ends up with an isolated d-pawn: a weakness in the endgame, but a source of space and free piece play in the middlegame. Tarrasch himself thought it was the only correct answer to the Queen’s Gambit.

    At 2000+ White scores only about 48% after 3...c5. Below 2000 many White players answer 4.cxd5 exd5 with 5.dxc5, and most then take back with 5...Bxc5, which leaves d5 short of defenders: 6.Qxd5 wins a pawn, and White scores about 59% after 5...Bxc5. At 2000+ about four in five play 5...d4 instead.

    What now: plans for both sides

    White

    • 4.cxd5 exd5 5.Nf3 Nc6 6.g3 is the main line: the g2 bishop hits d5 and White aims at the isolated pawn.
    • Trade pieces: the fewer pieces left, the weaker the isolated pawn becomes.
    • Blockade the pawn with a knight on d4, where no pawn can drive it away.

    Black

    • Keep pieces on and play actively: ...Nf6, ...Be7, ...O-O, ...Be6 and ...Re8.
    • The ...d4 push, when it works, gains space and cramps White.
    • The Schara Gambit 4...cxd4 gives a pawn for fast development; it is a sharp surprise.
