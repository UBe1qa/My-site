Plans for both sides

White

  • Against 2...Nf6, push 3.e5 Nd5 4.d4 and develop with Nf3, Bc4 or Bd3.
  • Against 2...d5, 3.exd5 Qxd5 4.d4 and the queen will be chased with Nc3 later.
  • You often get an isolated queen’s pawn on d4: use it for piece activity.

Black

  • 2...d5 and 2...Nf6 are the two best answers; both hit e4 before d4 arrives.
  • ...e6, ...Nc6 and ...cxd4 at the right moment are common in both lines.
  • If White gets an isolated pawn, blockade it on d5 and trade pieces.

Common traps

Delayed Alapin: 4...Nxe4?? 5.Qa4+

After 2.Nf3 d6 3.c3 Nf6 4.Be2 the e4 pawn looks free, but 4...Nxe4?? 5.Qa4+ checks the king and hits the knight at the same time. Between 1200 and 1599 about one in three players take it, and White then scores about 80%.

Fewer than 6 games in our data reached the end of this line.

At club level

Many club Sicilian players never study 2.c3 and answer with natural moves like 2...d6 3.d4, which hands White the full center. Two lines (2...d5 and 2...Nf6) are all Black needs.
