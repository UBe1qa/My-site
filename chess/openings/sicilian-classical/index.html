    For stronger players

    5...Nc6 is the most natural move in the Open Sicilian: the knight goes to its best square and hits d4. Black keeps the choice between ...e6, ...e5 and ...g6 for one more move, so White has to decide how to fight first.

    At 2000+ a third of players answer with 6.Bg5, and nearly half meet 6.Be3 with 6...Ng4, after which White scores only about 41%. Between 1200 and 1599 the most common move is 6.Bb5, and below 1200 it is 6.Nxc6. After 6.Nxc6 bxc6 White scores about 47%, so that trade helps Black.

    What now: plans for both sides

    White

    • 6.Bg5 (Richter-Rauzer) is the main line: after 6...e6 7.Qd2 White castles long and puts pressure on d6 and f6.
    • 6.Bc4 (Sozin) aims the bishop at e6 and f7. With Be3, Qe2 and O-O-O it becomes the very sharp Velimirovic Attack.
    • 6.Be3 is popular too, but 6...Ng4 hits the bishop at once. Answer it with 7.Bb5 or 7.Bg5.

    Black

    • Against 6.Bg5, 6...e6 with ...a6, ...Bd7 and ...Be7 is the main line. If White takes on f6, recapture with the g-pawn and use the bishop pair.
    • Against 6.Bc4, 6...e6 blunts the bishop, or 6...Qb6 (Benko) chases the d4 knight first.
    • After 6.Nxc6 bxc6 your center gets stronger: aim for ...e5 or ...d5 and use the half-open b-file.
