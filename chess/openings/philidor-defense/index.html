Plans for both sides

White

  • 3.d4 is the most testing move: it asks Black at once whether to hold e5 or give it up.
  • After 3...exd4 4.Nxd4 you have free development and more space.
  • Against slow setups, Bc4 and Ng5 ideas against f7 come up often.

Black

  • Choose a setup: ...exd4 and ...Nf6–...Be7 (open), or ...Nf6, ...Nbd7 and ...Be7 (Hanham, holding e5).
  • Do not let White’s bishop and knight gang up on f7 before you castle.
  • Pin the f3 knight with ...Bg4 only when e5 is safe; it is the start of Legal’s mate.

Common traps

Legal’s mate

With the knight pinned, 5.Nxe5! looks like it gives the queen away. If Black takes it with 5...Bxd1??, then 6.Bxf7+ Ke7 7.Nd5 is mate. After 5.Nxe5 Black should play 5...dxe5 6.Qxg4 and accept being a pawn down.

Fewer than 6 games in our data reached the end of this line.
