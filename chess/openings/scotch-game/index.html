    For stronger players

    3.d4 opens the center at once. White trades the d-pawn for Black’s e-pawn and gets a knight on d4 and free development. There is less theory than in the Ruy Lopez and the plans are easy to see.

    The Scotch is a great choice if you want an open game without learning the Ruy Lopez. Most club opponents answer 3...exd4 and then mix up the move order of ...Bc5 and ...Nf6, so knowing two plans is enough.

    What now: plans for both sides

    White

    • After 4.Nxd4 your knight sits in the center; meet ...Bc5 with Be3 or Nxc6, and ...Nf6 with Nxc6 and e5.
    • The e5 push drives Black’s knight away and gains space.
    • Castle short and use the open d-file for your rook.

    Black

    • 3...exd4 is the main move; 3...Nxd4? 4.Nxd4 exd4 5.Qxd4 gives White a big center.
    • 4...Bc5 or 4...Nf6 both develop with tempo against the d4 knight or e4.
    • The ...d5 break frees your game; after doubled c-pawns it fixes White’s e5 pawn.
