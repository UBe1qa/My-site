    For stronger players

    2...d6 prepares ...e5 without putting the bishop on g7 as in the King’s Indian. Black plays ...Nbd7, ...e5, ...Be7 and ...c6 for a solid, slightly cramped position that is hard to break.

    White answers 2...d6 with 3.Nc3 about three times in four. After 3...e5, the most common reply is 4.dxe5, and White scores only about 49% there. The English Rat (1.d4 d6 2.c4 e5) is similar: White scores about 47% at 2000+.

    What now: plans for both sides

    White

    • Nc3, e4 and Nf3 take the center; Be2 and O-O are natural.
    • Against an early ...e5, dxe5 dxe5 trades queens and leaves Black’s king in the center; d5 gains space instead.
    • Expand on the queenside with b4 and c5 once the center is closed.

    Black

    • ...Nbd7, ...e5, ...Be7, ...O-O and ...c6 is the classic setup.
    • You can still switch to a King’s Indian with ...g6 and ...Bg7.
    • The queen trade after dxe5 dxe5 Qxd8+ is not bad for Black: the position is level.
