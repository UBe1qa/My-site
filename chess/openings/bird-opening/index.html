Plans for both sides

White

  • Against 1...d5, Nf3, e3, Be2 or b3 with Bb2, and O-O is the classic setup; Ne5 and Qe1–h4 can follow.
  • Against From’s Gambit, 2.fxe5 d6 3.exd6 Bxd6 4.Nf3 is the safe line: with the f-pawn gone, never allow ...Qh4+.
  • Keep the e5 square: a knight there with f4 behind it is the heart of the Bird.

Black

  • 1...d5 with ...Nf6, ...g6 and ...Bg7, or ...Bg4, is solid against any setup.
  • From’s Gambit 1...e5 is sharp: after 4.Nf3 g5 Black throws the g-pawn at White’s king.
  • Aim at e4, which the f-pawn can no longer guard from f3, and contest e5 with ...Nc6 and ...e5.

Common traps

From’s Gambit: 4.Nc3?? Qh4+

After 3...Bxd6 White has to stop ...Qh4+. The natural 4.Nc3?? allows 4...Qh4+ 5.g3 Qxg3+ 6.hxg3 Bxg3 mate. 4.Nf3 covers h4 and is the main move.

Fewer than 6 games in our data reached the end of this line.

At club level

1...d5 is the most common answer at every level, about four games in ten. From’s Gambit comes in about one game in ten, and at 2000+ about four in five take with 2.fxe5. White scores about 52% after 1.f4 overall, but only about 49% below 1200.
