St. George Defense (1...a6): Lines and Stats by Rating

1...a6 prepares ...b5 and ...Bb7, a queenside fianchetto with extra space. It became famous when Tony Miles beat world champion Anatoly Karpov with it in 1980, but it gives White the center for free.

Plans for both sides

White

  • 2.d4 b5 3.Nf3 Bb7 4.Bd3 is a simple setup: protect e4 and develop.
  • 3.a4 or 3.c4 hits the b5 pawn at once and opens lines on the queenside.
  • Keep your center pawns supported; Black’s plan is to undermine them with ...c5 and ...e6.

Black

  • After 2.d4 b5, ...Bb7, ...e6, ...c5 and ...Nf6 hit e4 and d4.
  • Watch the b5 pawn: against a4 or c4 be ready with ...b4 or ...bxc4.
  • It is a surprise weapon; much of its success comes from opponents who do not know what to do.

At club level

Below 1200 White scores about 54% after 1...a6, but at 2000+ only about 49%. After 2.d4 b5, players below 2000 mostly play 3.Nf3, while at 2000+ the choices are spread between 3.Bd3, 3.Nf3, 3.c4 and 3.a4.
