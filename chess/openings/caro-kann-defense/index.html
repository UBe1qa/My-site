Plans for both sides

White

  • 3.e5 (Advance) is the most popular; meet ...Bf5 with Nf3, Be2 and O-O, or the sharp g4.
  • 3.Nc3 or 3.Nd2 dxe4 4.Nxe4 leads to the Classical: White gets space, Black a rock-solid position.
  • The Exchange (3.exd5 cxd5) and the Panov (4.c4) give open, active games.

Black

  • Get the bishop out with ...Bf5 or ...Bg4 before playing ...e6.
  • In the Advance, ...c5 is the key break; in the Classical, ...c5 or ...e5 frees the game.
  • In the 4...Nd7 lines, check for Nd6 tricks before you move the g8 knight (see the trap below).

Common traps

The Qe2 smothered mate

In the 4...Nd7 Classical, 5.Qe2 looks harmless. But 5...Ngf6?? 6.Nd6 is mate: the e7 pawn is pinned by the queen, so it cannot take the knight, and the king has no square. Black plays 5...Ndf6 instead.
