Polish Opening (1.b4): 1...e5, 1...d5, a Trap and Stats by Rating

1.b4 grabs queenside space and prepares Bb2 toward e5 and g7. It is also known as the Sokolsky or the Orangutan. The b4 pawn is loose, so Black can gain time by attacking it, but the opening sets real problems for players who rarely meet it.

Plans for both sides

White

  • Against 1...e5, 2.Bb2 hits e5 at once; after 2...Bxb4 3.Bxe5 White has traded the b-pawn for the e-pawn.
  • a3 or b5 keeps the pawn when Black attacks it with ...a5, or with ...c6 and ...Qb6.
  • e3, Nf3, c4 and Be2 complete the setup; the b2 bishop is your key piece.

Black

  • 1...e5 2.Bb2 Bxb4 is the most direct line; after 3.Bxe5, play 3...Nf6 to block the diagonal.
  • 1...e5 with ...d6 or ...f6 keeps the center and blunts the b2 bishop.
  • 1...d5 with ...Nf6, ...e6 or ...Bf5 is a solid, quiet answer.

Common traps

3...Nc6? 4.Bxg7

After 2...Bxb4 3.Bxe5 the g7 pawn hangs. 3...Nf6 blocks the diagonal; the natural 3...Nc6? loses g7, and the h8 rook is next. White scores about 80% after 3...Nc6.

At club level

After 1...e5 2.Bb2, players at 2000+ most often take with 2...Bxb4, but below 1200 2...Nc6 is the top reply. After 2...Bxb4 3.Bxe5, nine in ten at 2000+ play 3...Nf6, while below 1200 only about one in eight does, and White scores about 67% there. White scores about 53% after 1.b4 overall.
