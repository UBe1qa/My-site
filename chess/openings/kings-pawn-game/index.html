    For stronger players

    1...e5 mirrors White’s first move and leads to the open games. White’s second move decides the family: 2.Nf3 is played in about six games in ten, then 2.Bc4, 2.Nc3, 2.d4 and 2.f4. This page also covers the rare second moves that have names of their own.

    Below 1200, 2.Qh5 is played in about one game in sixteen after 1.e4 e5; at 2000+ the five main moves cover almost everything, with 2.Nf3 at about 71%.

    What now: plans for both sides

    White

    • 2.Nf3 attacks e5 and develops; it leads to the Italian, Ruy Lopez, Scotch, Four Knights and more.
    • 2.Bc4, 2.Nc3, 2.f4 and 2.d4 are the Bishop’s Opening, Vienna, King’s Gambit and Center Game, each with its own guide.
    • Quiet moves like 2.d3, 2.c3 or 2.Ne2 are playable but give Black an easy game with ...d5 or ...Nf6.

    Black

    • Against 2.Nf3, 2...Nc6 is by far the most common reply; 2...Nf6 (Petrov) and 2...d6 (Philidor) are the solid alternatives.
    • Against slow second moves, take the center with ...d5 or develop with ...Nf6 and ...Bc5.
    • Against the early queen moves 2.Qh5 and 2.Qf3, defend f7 first and develop with tempo against the queen.
