How this counts

Words
Runs of text separated by spaces or line breaks, so “well-known” and “don't” are one word each. The Unicode-rules figure splits at hyphens and also works for scripts written without spaces.
Characters
One for each character as people see it (grapheme clusters, Unicode UAX #29). An emoji such as 👨‍👩‍👧 is 1, however many pieces it is built from.
Spaces
Spaces, tabs and line breaks. “Without spaces” leaves all of them out.
Line breaks
A line break counts as 0, 1 or 2 characters, whichever you choose. It starts at 1.
Sentences
Unicode sentence boundaries: after a full stop, question mark or exclamation mark, and at line breaks.
Paragraphs
Lines that contain text. Blank lines are not counted.
Reading time
Words divided by a reading speed. The starting values are averages for adults reading English from Brysbaert's 2019 review of 190 studies: 238 words per minute for silent reading of non-fiction and 183 for reading aloud. Most adults fall between 175 and 300 for silent reading, so set your own pace under “How this counts”.

Questions

Does the character count include spaces?

“Characters” includes spaces and line breaks. “Without spaces” leaves out spaces, tabs and line breaks. Both are shown side by side.

Why does another counter show a different number?

Usually because of line breaks, hyphenated words or emoji. Some tools count a line break as two characters, some split “well-known” into two words, and some count an emoji by its internal pieces instead of as one character. Open “How this counts” to switch the line-break rule.

Is my text uploaded or saved?

No. Counting happens in your browser. This site does not send your text to a server or store it, and it is gone when you close the page. Ad and visit-statistics requests do go out, but your text is not part of them.

How is reading time worked out?

It is the word count divided by a reading speed. The starting value of 238 words per minute is the average for adults silently reading English non-fiction in a 2019 review of 190 studies (Brysbaert), and 183 is the average for reading aloud. They are averages, not a measurement of you, so set your own pace under “How this counts”.
