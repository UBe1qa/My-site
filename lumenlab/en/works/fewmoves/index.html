Arrow thickness is how often players at that rating choose the move, read from the same 175 million Lichess games as Opening Trail.

Pick your rating and see the moves your opponents really play

  1. Pick your ratingTap your level under ‘Lichess rating’ on the Map, and the table and arrows switch to the moves players at that level chose, with how often.
  2. Play a moveMove a piece or tap a move in the table to go to the next position. Every move is labelled main, alternative or mistake, and named positions show the opening name and a link to its guide.
  3. Read a guideOpen one of the 81 guides under ‘Openings’ to follow the main line with a reason for every move, then traps, plans for both sides, variations and a table of replies by rating.
  4. Test and practiceSolve 10 positions in about 2 minutes under ‘Test’ to see your level. Press ‘Practice as White’ or ‘Practice as Black’ at the end of a guide; your opponent answers with moves really played at your rating, and wrong answers go into your review.

What it checks and discloses

The numbers are our own count of the August to October 2026 rated games Lichess publishes, the main, alternative and mistake labels come from engine scores in the Lichess evaluation database, and every line in the guides is played through with chess.js when the site is built, with traps marked as mate checked to be checkmate.

Rules and sources

Games
We counted 174,931,775 blitz, rapid and classical games from the whole August, September and October 2026 rated games files Lichess publishes. Bullet, ultrabullet and correspondence are left out.Lichess game database (CC0) · Checked Nov 5, 2026
Rating levels
Games are grouped by the average of both ratings: under 1200, 1200–1599, 1600–1999 and 2000+. These are Lichess ratings, which usually run higher than Chess.com ratings for the same player.Opening Trail data and sources · Checked Nov 5, 2026
What is shown
The first 20 moves (40 plies), keeping move orders seen in more than 20 games. The main, alternative and mistake labels come from engine scores in the Lichess evaluation database (CC0).Opening Trail data and sources · Checked Nov 5, 2026
Opening names
Names and ECO codes come from the chess-openings list Lichess publishes (3,865 entries, CC0).lichess-org/chess-openings · Checked Nov 4, 2026
Guide lines
Every main line, variation and trap in the guides is played move by move with chess.js each time the site is built, and traps marked ‘ends in mate’ are checked to end in checkmate.chess.js · Checked Nov 4, 2026
What stays on your device
Your rating level, language and sound settings and practice record are kept in this browser only and never sent to a server. There is no sign-up or login.Opening Trail Privacy Policy (the four things it stores) · Checked Nov 5, 2026
