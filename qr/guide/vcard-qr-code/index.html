vCard QR codes: what goes inside and how it saves

Last checked October 14, 2026

A vCard QR code stores contact details such as your name, phone and email as vCard text inside the image. When someone scans it, the phone shows an "Add contact" screen and they tap Save. Every field you add makes the code denser, so for a business card, stick to the essentials: name, phone and email.

What exactly is stored?

These are the lines the vCard QR code generator produces for Alex Kim with a company, job title, mobile number and email. Lines are separated by CRLF.

Stored lineMeaning
BEGIN:VCARD · VERSION:3.0Start of a vCard 3.0
N:Kim;Alex;;;Last name; first name
FN:Alex KimDisplay name
ORG:Staycode Studio · TITLE:OwnerCompany and job title
TEL;TYPE=CELL,VOICE:+15551234567Mobile; spaces and dashes are removed
EMAIL;TYPE=INTERNET:alex@example.comEmail
END:VCARDEnd

Swipe the table sideways

Add more and you get URL: (website), ADR;TYPE=WORK: (address) and NOTE: lines. "Add another phone" adds work, home or fax numbers as separate lines such as TEL;TYPE=WORK. Commas and semicolons in your text are escaped with a backslash, lines longer than 75 bytes are folded as vCard requires, and accented or non-Latin names are stored as UTF-8.

The full card is version 13, so the generator shows its "dense code" warning (it appears from version 10). For a small card, leave out the address and note, or build a contact page and put its link in a URL code. The trade-off with a URL code: the person has to open the page, and the "Add contact" screen does not appear by itself.

What happens on the phone?

When the phone recognizes a vCard, it opens an "Add contact" screen with name, phones, email, company, title, address and note filled into their own fields. Nothing is saved until the person scanning taps Save. The screen can look different depending on the phone and camera app, so scan your own code before printing to check every field lands in the right place. The QR code scanner also shows the fields a code contains.

Printing it on a business card

  • Size: a common rule of thumb is at least 2 cm (0.8 in) for close-up scans. Go bigger for a dense card, or cut fields. The full table is in QR code print size.
  • Files: give your printer SVG or PDF so it stays sharp at any size. PDF sizes refer to the pattern itself, without the quiet zone or frame.
  • Color: to match your card, change the dot color and keep the background light. Check that the stamp says it scans.
  • Trademark line: according to DENSO WAVE's FAQ (checked October 14, 2026), you do not need a trademark notice when you only print the code image on a business card, and commercial use needs no fee or license. The notice is only asked for when you use the words "QR Code".

Contact details are processed in your browser only and are never kept in "Recent QR codes".

vCard or a link?

A vCard code works offline and saves the contact in one tap, which suits trade shows and printed cards. A link code stays small and lets you update details later on your own page, but needs a connection and an extra step to save. One option is to put the vCard on the printed card and the link on your website or email signature.
